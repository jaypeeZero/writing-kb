# Revision Notes — "These are the things that I remember"

Working list. The unifying principle behind every item: **perform the move, don't cite it.**

---

## 1. "I remember" — vary the vehicle for memorial distance

The emotional distance to memory is the right frame. The execution leans on a single phrase ~15 times, which flattens it into a tic. Distance is a tone, not a chant.

Keep "I remember" three or four times, placed for stress. Carry the distance in other registers the rest of the time:

- **Tense shift.** Past for the framing voice, present for the embedded scene. The shift itself signals "this is memory."
- **Reportorial flatness.** Just narrate cold. The flatness is the distance.
- **Texture the memory itself.** *"The cabin had — a green couch, a yellow lamp, things I'd swear to in court and might be making up."* One sentence does the work of ten "I remember"s.
- **Memory as a character in the prose.** *"The road back is clearer to me than the house. Why I'm not sure."*

Litany only works when each iteration carries different freight (cf. O'Brien's "they carried"). If the phrase stays, each instance has to pull different weight.

---

## 2. "I don't remember" — occupy the absence, don't cite it

The blanks are load-bearing. The forgetting is significant — Sebald, Carson, Offill all built work on this. The principle: **occupy the absence; don't cite it.** Make the shape of the forgetting specific so the reader feels the blank instead of being told it exists.

Examples of the move:

- Cited: *"I don't remember what else they said."*
- Occupied: *"What they said next is gone. I can see Bonner's mouth moving and my mother's voice in my ear, and between them, nothing."*

- Cited: *"I don't remember the rest of the conversation."*
- Occupied: *"There's a gap here — the hour between the call ending and the engine starting. I've been told what I did in it. I don't recognize the boy described."*

- Carson-style fragment: *"After that: a sound, maybe a door. I have lost the room."*

The shape of what's missing — where it begins, where it ends, what's around its edges — is where the feeling lives.

---

## 3. "He was a smart kid" — dramatize the arrival, don't caption

Intent is the present-self realizing it. The line currently reads as résumé caption because the prose doesn't make the realization *happen* on the page. Small fix: let the arrival be visible.

- Caption: *"He was a smart kid."*
- Arrival: *"I see it now — he was a smart kid. The phone call. Who thinks of that at ten?"*
- Quieter: *"Smart kid, I think now. Ten years old and he thought of the phone."*

Same content. The prose admits where it's standing.

Apply the same fix to "He was a scrappy kid…" — that whole portrait paragraph arrives after Bonner's arc is over. Either move pieces of it earlier (so it lives next to action), or reframe it as present-day realization rather than past-tense description.

---

## 4. "yes I said that" — cut the wink

This is the hardest one. The wink is the present-self asking forgiveness on behalf of the past-self. If at 17 you actually believed "we're good Christian kids" was a moral shield, the braver move is to **let the line stand naked**, with no parenthetical apology, and trust the reader to register the irony.

The wink protects the writer. Cutting it leaves the 17-year-old's belief intact and lets the irony arrive via the reader — which is where it has to arrive to land.

If you want present-self presence here, do it later, with weight — as a reckoning, not a parenthetical. One option: the close becomes the place the older self stands. Don't apologize mid-scene; let the cost surface after.

---

## 5. The boy — place a detail, step back

This is the spine. You named the right problem: interpretation = melodrama. The fix is **placement**, not commentary.

The materials are already in the text, just buried mid-paragraph:

- the trails worn narrow through the woods, **made by the boy**
- ten-year-old hands stretched like an NBA player's, rock the size of his sister's head
- the sister's one sentence: *"could they go home"*
- the grandmother's *"might make things worse"*

Any one of these, returned at the close, lands without melodrama. Options to try:

> We drove home. Sometimes when I'm in the woods and I see a path worn narrow between the trees, I think about who might have made it.

> We drove home. I still think about that shotgun sometimes. I think about the boy's hands more.

> We drove home. I never knew what happened to him.

Principle: when you don't know how to say what you felt, place a detail and step back. The detail is the feeling. "I felt helpless" is melodrama. *"I never knew what happened to him"* is the same helplessness, carried by the reader, sitting in their chest instead of on the page.

The crushed feeling isn't melodramatic when written — it's melodramatic when named. The not-naming is what makes it literary.

Bonus: the sister's *"could they go home"* is structurally placed like a Chekhov's gun and never fires. It wants to come back.

---

## 6. "In the middle of nowhere" — vary the freight

Intent (loneliness, distance, safety stake) is sound. Eleven instances of the same phrase doing the same work flattens the loneliness into sameness. Keep the phrase; make each instance pull different freight. Rough sketch of distinct jobs:

- one **sensory** (the silence; the trees holding the only sound)
- one about **distance from help** (this lands the safety stake — the shotgun later)
- one about **disconnection** (cell signal, the world unreachable)
- one about the **cabin's particular kind of nowhere** (the mobile-home recognition is here)
- one about **scale** (the woods around, the dark beyond the dark)

Vary the sentence around it so each repetition adds rather than echoes. The phrase becomes a refrain that builds instead of a tic that wears.

---

## 7. Pacing — slow time at the flash

The proportional disequilibrium (lots of road, little event) matches how the memory sits, and that's correct. The move the prose isn't yet making is **expansion at the climax** — which doesn't mean *length*, it means *time slowing*.

A flash can be three short paragraphs that take five seconds of in-world time. Tolstoy's Anna gets pages for seconds. The sentence-by-sentence *register* changes at the shotgun moment even if the moment itself is brief. Currently the prose breathes the same way at the gravel road and at the shotgun.

Things to try at the shotgun beat:

- fragment sentences
- drop into present tense for the duration of the flash
- strip interiority (the colored eye narrows; only inventory remains)
- the "Blue and white linoleum. Brown wood. Brushed bronze." list is already doing this — let it expand, give it its own breath
- white space — a one-line paragraph or two — to force pauses around it

The flash quality is preserved; the prose register registers that it *is* a flash.

---

## 8. Dialogue — action beats around what was said

Memoir constraint accepted: this is what was said. But the literary work can still happen in:

- **which lines you include.** You don't owe the reader every word; pick the ones doing work.
- **action beats between lines.** What the mother's voice sounded like at *"How many are at the party?"* What her hand was doing. The pause before *"You've been drinking?"*
- **the narrator's internal response** registering what the line is doing. The mother's questions reveal she's been on this kind of call before — that other kids have been here drinking — that this is known terrain to her. One beat of the narrator clocking that does the power-shift work.

The dialogue stays. The work happens in the space around it.

---

## Net

Most of these are small. They share one revision: stop citing the move; perform it. Memorial distance → perform it in tense and texture, not in repeated phrase. Memory blanks → perform them by shaping the absence, not naming it. Realizations → perform the arrival, not the caption. Irony → perform it by letting the bare line stand, not winking. The boy → perform him by placement, not interpretation. Loneliness → perform it by varied freight, not refrain. The flash → perform it by changing prose tempo, not duration. Dialogue subtext → perform it in the beats, not the lines.

Same gap on every item. Closing it is the revision.
